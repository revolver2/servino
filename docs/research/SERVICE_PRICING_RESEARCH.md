**Read this warning before trusting any single number below:** most
consumer-facing "cost guide" sites are SEO content written to rank for
searches like "wall panel cost Toronto," not primary market data. Several
numbers below come from the same handful of blog/guide sites repackaging

minimum-job/manual-review guardrails rather than trusting any one figure

## 6. Ontario sales tax (HST)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Source | **Canada Revenue Agency** (official), directly fetched rate table |
| URL | https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html |
| Retrieved | 2026-09-23 |
| Geography | Ontario, Canada (official federal source) |
| Quoted rate | **13%** HST, unchanged since July 2010; CRA's own historical rate table confirms 13% for every period from April 2013 through "on or after April 1, 2025" (no scheduled change) |
| Unit | Percentage of taxable supply |
| Labor/material | Applies to the full taxable supply (both labor and material, when Servino is the entity supplying the service — see the marketplace-obligation caveat below) |
| Limitations | None on the rate itself — this is a primary government source, not a guide-site aggregation |
| Confidence | **HIGH — this is the one figure in this document that needs no professional/accountant validation.** It matches the value already hardcoded in `dev-pricing-config.ts` (`DEV_ONTARIO_HST_RATE = 0.13`), confirming that pre-existing assumption was correct. |

**⚠ REQUIRES BUSINESS/TAX CONFIRMATION — marketplace HST obligation.** The 13% *rate* is certain; **who is legally obligated to charge and remit it is not**, and depends on Servino's actual contractual relationship with professionals (is Servino the supplier of record charging the customer directly, with professionals paid as subcontractors — the current app model — or could professionals ever be the supplier of record with Servino only facilitating?). Research confirms:
- The federal **small-supplier threshold is $30,000** in trailing-12-month taxable revenue — below that, a business is not required to register for/charge GST/HST (CRA, https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html, retrieved 2026-09-23). This matters for both Servino itself and for any individual professional who might separately invoice.
- Canada's 2021 "digital platform" GST/HST rules primarily target **non-resident vendors and platforms** selling digital products/short-term accommodation into Canada — they do not cleanly describe a Canadian-resident marketplace connecting Canadian customers with Canadian in-person trade labor (Stripe's own guide and the CRA's digital-economy pages, retrieved 2026-09-23). Servino's situation is closer to an ordinary "who is the supplier for HST purposes" question than a digital-platform-rules question.
- **This document does not resolve that question and Session E's implementation does not either.** The current architecture (customer pays Servino's quoted total; Servino separately compensates professionals) is consistent with Servino being the supplier of record, which is the assumption the pricing engine bakes in — but this is exactly the kind of structural decision that needs real accountant/lawyer sign-off before real money moves, not an AI research summary. Flagged in `docs/PRICING_MODEL.md` and `docs/UNIT_ECONOMICS.md`.

## Questions to Validate With First Professionals

Internet research cannot replace direct conversation with the actual
1–4 professionals Servino launches with. Before treating Session E's
launch rates as final, the owner should ask each professional:

1. What's the **minimum job you'll accept**, in dollars — below what
   size does a job simply not make sense for you to travel for?
2. For a **typical 10 m² wall** (roughly a large feature wall), what
   would you charge for **installation-only** labor, assuming the
   customer already has panels/wallpaper/material on-site?
3. If Servino supplies material handling (ordering, delivery
   coordination), does that change your labor price at all?
4. What's your **realistic travel radius** within the GTA, and does
   price change for jobs outside central Toronto (parking, congestion,
   drive time)?
5. What **wall preparation** do you consider included vs. billable
   separately (patching small holes, minor sanding, removing old
   wallpaper)?
6. What **waste percentage** do you actually plan for on panel/
   wallpaper material — pattern matching, cutting, damaged pieces? Does
   it differ meaningfully between wallpaper, panels, and marble/faux
   sheets?
7. How do you handle **outlets, switches, corners, and other
   obstacles** — extra time, flat add-on, or included?
8. Is **parking** in downtown/dense Toronto neighborhoods something you
   bill separately, and roughly how much?
9. Do you expect **cleanup/debris removal** to be included in your
   quoted price, or billed separately?
10. What's a realistic **revisit/callback** expectation if something
    needs touching up after the main job — included goodwill, or a
    separate billable visit?

None of these have been asked yet (Session E is architecture, not
outreach) — this list exists so the *next* real conversation with a
professional is structured and comparable across all of them, not ad
hoc.

## Deliberately not modeled in Session E

Per this phase's own instruction not to build a complexity engine for
the first 10 jobs: **wall condition, ceiling height, demolition
requirement, and electrical/obstacle count** are not captured as
pricing inputs, because the customer funnel doesn't currently collect
them and inventing defaults would be false precision. Where research
above shows these meaningfully move price (e.g. wallpaper prep
surcharge, marble installation complexity), Session E's pricing model
handles the risk by keeping the **minimum-job floor** and a
**manual-review path** for anything the deterministic formula can't
safely price, rather than guessing at a complexity multiplier with no
evidence behind it.
